# ICP — crystallised (08 Sep 2026)

## Locked ICP: the public skill-repo publisher

**One sentence:** a solo builder or very small team who has published their own working Claude Skills as a public GitHub repo, for reasons that have nothing to do with Atlan — audience, credibility, or teaching — and who now has forks and strangers depending on a repo they maintain alone.

## Exact profile

- **Org type:** not really an "org." Either (a) a single individual — an indie developer, technical educator, or solo consultant — publishing under their own GitHub handle, or (b) a very small team (2-10 people) at an early-stage startup where one engineer publishes the company's internal skill library as a public repo.
- **Team size:** 1 (solo) to 10. No IT department, no procurement, no manager approving tool spend. The person who wrote the skill is the person who decides whether to keep maintaining it.
- **Day-to-day work:** they use Claude Code or Cowork every day to do their actual job — shipping a SaaS product, writing client work, producing tutorials/courses. The skills in the repo are literally their accumulated "how I work" instructions, refined over months of real use, not written as a demo for the repo.
- **Tools used today:** Claude Code / Claude Cowork (daily driver), GitHub (public, personal or small-org account), a distribution channel for their own name — X/Twitter, a newsletter, YouTube, a blog. Often Next.js/TypeScript or similar for indie devs; Notion/HubSpot-adjacent tools for marketing operators.
- **BYO-agent, confirmed:** pays for Claude out of pocket or on a personal/startup card. No enterprise plan, no admin console, no one else's sign-off required to adopt a new tool.
- **Why they published publicly:** not altruism — it's audience-building. The repo is content marketing for their real business (a course, a consulting practice, a newsletter, a personal brand that drives their startup's visibility).
- **The moment they're in when we can reach them:** the repo already has real traction (dozens to hundreds of forks) or they're about to publish one. They're actively thinking about the repo's credibility and quality because their name is on it.

## Two concrete examples (real, already in the research)

1. **The indie technical educator** — in the shape of mattpocock/skills (257k stars, 19k forks). A solo developer/educator publishing the Claude Skills they use in their own TypeScript workflow, promoted to their existing audience, in service of a paid product (course, consulting, sponsorship) they sell separately. No company. No team. Just them and their GitHub handle.

2. **The solo growth/marketing consultant** — in the shape of coreyhaines31/marketingskills (48k stars, 6.9k forks, still climbing). A marketing operator publishing the Claude Skills they use for content and campaign work, to build a personal brand that feeds a newsletter, community, or consulting practice. This is the sub-segment that answers Atlan's "marketing/sales" signal without us inventing a separate ICP for it.

## Why this is the ICP, in plain terms

- The population is countable, not estimated: 257k / 211k / 48k stars and 19k / 6.9k / 69 forks across three named repos we pulled live on 8 Sep 2026.
- The loop is proven, not guessed: shields.io badges already sit in 1.2M READMEs and serve 1B+ requests a month. A fork copies the README mechanically — no one has to be persuaded to share it.
- BYO-agent is built into the definition — there is no procurement step to design around.
- It covers both the CEO's engineering signal and marketing/sales signal without splitting into two different products.
- What we ruled out and why: legal and HR searches came back empty (checked, not just unexplored); agencies had the strongest pain (a client offering $4,000/month) but the loop there is borrowed from Clutch/G2 by analogy, not observed, and agentman.ai already ships that exact mechanic.
