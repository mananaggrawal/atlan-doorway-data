# Find the first 100 teams for Atlan Agent Registry

## The role
You are responsible for early go-to-market for Atlan Agent Registry.

Treat this exercise like your first week in the role. We are looking for someone who can absorb an unfamiliar product, find a sharp wedge, build with AI, and create momentum without waiting for a complete playbook.

This is a substantial work sample. The brief defines the floor, not the ceiling. We have intentionally left room for you to decide what exceptional work looks like.

## The product
Atlan Registry (name TBD) a new product that Atlan is launching in our quest to become a context layer for AI. It is a governed home for the context behind agents.

Two agents running the same model can behave like different systems because their skills, instructions, tools, knowledge, permissions, and environments differ. Most teams still manage that context through copied files, hardcoded configuration, and disconnected sessions. They often cannot tell which version ran, who owns it, what it can access, or what happened when people used it.

Registry gives these objects a stable identity and connects them to owners, versions, access, dependencies, evals, usage, and traces. Teams can discover and distribute governed context into supported agent harnesses such as Claude Code and Codex while continuing to use Git, identity systems, and observability tools for the jobs they already do.

The initial wedge is skills. Skills already move among laptops, repositories, CI, Claude Code, Codex, and cloud runtimes. A useful skill can begin as one person's workflow, become a team standard, and eventually become governed company infrastructure.

## Our starting thesis
We want dramatic distribution before we optimize for a traditional enterprise sales motion.

Our current ICP hypothesis includes individuals and teams setting up Claude Cowork, Claude Code, Codex, or similar agent harnesses. We see a smaller personal use case and a larger team or workspace use case. We realise that these workloads today are split between: Coding workload, Knowledge worker productivity workload, and operational agentic workloads. We do not yet know which should lead, how one expands into the other, or which channel can compound fastest.

Possible channels include individual builder communities (e.g n8n/ clay's distribution), leveraging public skill directories (marketplace for skills?), open source, platform partnerships (e.g. AWS recently launched agent registry. Can we growth hack that?), native agent recommendations (Can claude setup and recommend registry?), creators, agencies, and service partners. These are just an initial list of prompts, not conclusions. You may reject all of them.

## What you will have access to
You will receive the product materials linked below, plus the internal recordings shared with the exercise. You have access to our team, and can send in questions, request calls - basically act like you would if this was your first week on the job!

## Your challenge
Build and begin testing a credible path to the first 100 activated teams for Atlan Agent Registry.

Choose one initial ICP and one primary distribution channel. Go deep enough to show why this combination can work now. Then execute the first GTM experiment campaign and build something that advances the bet.

We care about three outcomes:
1. You find a specific group with a painful, current problem.
2. You choose a channel that matches how that group already discovers and adopts tools.
3. You ship evidence that moves the idea beyond a strategy deck.

### 1. Choose the first customer and gather evidence
Define the narrowest credible early adopter. Name the job they are trying to do, the trigger that makes the problem urgent, what they use today, and why Agent Registry is meaningfully better for this job.

Map the usage and adoption system. Who is the user, champion, blocker, and budget owner? What channels do they typically find products on? How could individual adoption become team or workspace adoption? Who should not be targeted yet?

If you speak with people, use your own network or an appropriate public community. Explain that you are completing a candidate exercise.

### 2. Choose one channel
Pick one primary channel and defend the choice. Explain why it fits the customer's existing behavior, how discovery leads to activation and expansion, what Atlan must build or partner on, and why this route beats the two strongest alternatives. A list of plausible channels is not a strategy.

We are especially interested in distribution channels with non-linear scale, but also feel free to pick the channel that you are most comfortable executing. The goal is to test your depth, and execution bar in the next phase of the challenge.

### 3. Execute the first campaign and build something
Create the first campaign for your chosen channel. It should be ready to run, not described in future tense.

Show the audience, offer, channel-native acquisition asset, destination or activation experience, call to action, launch mechanics, and measurement plan. Define the signal that would make you continue, change, or stop.

Leave behind something we can click, run, inspect, or put in front of a user. Examples include:
- a working skill, skill pack, or installation flow that creates a distribution loop;
- a landing page with a concrete offer and activation path;
- a public-directory or marketplace concept with a working submission or discovery experience;
- a partner package and technical proof for an AWS, Anthropic, OpenAI, or adjacent-platform motion;
- a small tool or agent that finds, evaluates, distributes, or maintains skills;
- an outbound campaign ready to launch

These are examples, not a menu. Pick the artifact your thesis requires. A polished mockup with no path to evidence is weaker than a rough working system that teaches us something.

### 4. Show how you worked with AI
We expect you to be AI-native. Use AI aggressively for research, synthesis, building, design, coding, content, analysis, and iteration wherever it improves the work.

Ask your AI to share with us a short AI work log covering:
- your approach to AI, and models/ harnesses/ agents you used
- the systems or reusable workflows you built;
- one place where the AI was wrong, shallow, or misleading;
- the decision you made that the AI could not make for you.

We are not scoring the number of tools. We are looking for output multiplied by AI, sound judgment, and an operating style that outperforms a conventional workflow.

## What to submit
Your core submission should be understandable in 15 minutes. Use the format that best serves your argument. Appendices can go deeper.

Submit:
1. A decision document or deck with your ICP, channel bet, campaign, distribution loop, activation definition, and next experiments.
2. The thing you built, with a link, repository, file, recording, or clear run instructions.
3. An evidence appendix with sources, discovery notes, assumptions, and rejected alternatives.
4. Your AI work log.

We will use a 50-minute working session:
- 20 minutes for your readout and demo;
- 20 minutes for questions and pushback;
- 10 minutes to change or extend one part of the work together.

There is no prescribed time limit, but we are budgeting 5-7 calendar days to setup the working session. That open-endedness is deliberate. We expect a serious work sample, visible execution, and evidence that you cared about the outcome. We will evaluate the judgment, learning, and shipped work your effort produced rather than the number of hours you report.

Please know that we will not use your submission commercially.
